# SIPOC process overview
## Supplier · Input · Process · Output · Customer

# This is how you work with this template

- Use SIPOC to understand the scope and dependencies of one process, not to record every action.
- Use the help and the example row as a checklist; enter your answers on **Process boundaries** and in the empty **SIPOC** matrix.
- Preferably work from customer to supplier, with nouns for input and output and verbs for process steps.
- Only slides labeled **Skipped** will be left out of presentation and export. Turn **Skip** on or off for explanations that your audience may or may not need.

---

# What does SIPOC map?

- **Supplier:** provides the information or resources the process needs.
- **Input:** data, materials or other conditions required by the process.
- **Process:** 4 to 7 high-level activities that transform the input.
- **Output:** the product, service or information the process produces.
- **Customer:** the internal or external recipient of the output.

# Checklist — When are the boundaries clear enough?

- **Process:** give it a recognizable name with verb and subject, for example “Register order”.
- **Starting point:** Name one observable event, for example “Request received”.
- **Endpoint:** name one demonstrable result, for example “Order confirmation sent”.
- Choose boundaries around which the team can make meaningful agreements.
- Move exceptions and adjacent processes outside the matrix; write them down separately.

---

<!-- skip -->

# Checklist — Complete from right to left

1. Set clear start and end points for the process.
2. Name the customers who depend on the result.
3. Describe the outputs they receive.
4. Summarise the process in 4 to 7 high-level activities.
5. Determine which inputs those activities need.
6. Link each input to the supplier who makes it available.

# SIPOC or a detailed flowchart?

| Characteristic | SIPOC | Detailed flowchart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Define scope and relationships | Document work and decisions |
| Detail | 4 to 7 high-level activities | May contain dozens of steps |
| Focus | Suppliers, inputs, outputs and customers | Sequence, hand-offs and decision points |
| Use | Start of an improvement effort | Execution and fault analysis |
