# Setting boundaries
## With whom · About what · Date

---

# What I want to achieve

- For myself: state my boundary clearly and calmly
- For the other person: make it understandable, not a rejection
- For the relationship: keep respect, even when I say no

<!-- _class: section -->

# The approach: having a crucial conversation

---

# Start with the heart — what do I really want?

- For myself: what goal am I serving with this conversation?
- For the other person: what do I wish for them?
- For the relationship: how do I want us to treat each other afterwards?
- Don't lose sight of that goal once things get tense

---

# Separate the facts from your story

- Fact: what actually happened or was said, observably?
- Story: what explanation or assumption have I attached to it?
- Ask yourself: why would a reasonable person do this?
- Avoid the victim, villain, and helpless stories

---

# Make it safe and keep it safe

- Share a common goal: what do we want to achieve together?
- Show genuine respect, even when you disagree
- When there's a misunderstanding, contrast: what you don't mean, and what you do
- Watch for silence (withdrawal) or aggression (forcing) as a safety signal

---

# Share your path — STATE

- Share the facts: start neutral and verifiable
- Tell your story: what you think about it, delivered carefully
- Ask about the other person's path: invite their view
- Talk tentatively and encourage differing views

---

# Explore the other person's path — listen (AMPP)

- Ask: pose an open question and mean it
- Mirror: name what you see ("you seem affected")
- Paraphrase: summarize what you hear in your own words
- Prime: gently suggest what might be going on if things stall

---

# How I'll say it

- Name the behavior and its effect, briefly and concretely
- State what you need, using "I" statements
- Stay friendly but repeat your boundary if needed
- Offer an alternative where possible
