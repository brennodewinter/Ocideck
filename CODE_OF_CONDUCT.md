# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Just Culture

Just Culture is one of the core values of Stichting LibreKAT — alongside *de
menselijke maat*, the human dimension — so we state it here rather than leave it
implied. The Contributor Covenant below is our base; this section is where the
foundation's own values are written down. We build security tooling, and that
work depends on people being willing to say "I broke it", "I don't understand
this", or "I found something bad" — out loud and early. A community that punishes
those sentences does not get safer; it only gets quieter.

In practice:

- **Mistakes are information, not offences.** When something goes wrong — a
  broken build, a leaked secret, a bad merge, a confident answer that was wrong —
  the first question is *what happened and what allowed it to happen*, not *who
  is to blame*.
- **We fix systems before we correct people.** If a mistake was easy to make, the
  design made it easy. Guard rails, tests, checks, and clearer documentation are
  the preferred response.
- **Reporting is protected.** Anyone who reports their own error, someone else's,
  or a weakness in the project in good faith is thanked, not penalised. That
  holds for vulnerabilities in OciDeck itself (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md))
  and just as much for missteps inside the community.
- **We distinguish honest error, risky habit, and reckless choice.** An honest
  mistake is met with support. A pattern of at-risk behaviour is met with a
  conversation and better guard rails. A deliberate or reckless disregard for
  other people's safety, privacy, or trust is met with the enforcement measures
  in this document.
- **Just Culture is not the absence of accountability.** It replaces blame for
  outcomes with responsibility for behaviour and for repair: understanding the
  harm, putting it right, and improving whatever made it possible.

Its companion value is *de menselijke maat* — the human dimension. We deal with a
person, not a case number: we look at the situation someone was actually in,
leave room for circumstances a rule never anticipated, and keep our procedures in
proportion to what is at stake. Process serves people here, not the other way
around, and "the rules say so" is never the end of a conversation.

Nobody is exempt, maintainers least of all. Leaders who cannot own their own
mistakes reduce this section to a slogan.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall
  community.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of
  any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of
acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in
response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive,
or harmful.

Corrective action follows [Just Culture](#just-culture): the response is
proportionate to the behaviour and to what the situation made likely, not to how
severe the outcome happened to look in hindsight.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when
an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement by e-mail to
**security@vigilis.nl**. All complaints will be reviewed and
investigated promptly and fairly. Community leaders are obligated to respect the
privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.
